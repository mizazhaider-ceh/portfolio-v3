# Portfolio v2, Design Review (Designer lens)

**Reviewer:** independent design reviewer (visual + UX, design systems)
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Site reviewed:** http://localhost:4173/ (preview server, build as served)
**Source:** `~/workspace/portfolio-v2/` (React 19 + TS + Vite + Tailwind + react-router)
**Grounding read:** `plan/DESIGN-PLAN.md`, `research/CONTENT-MODEL.md`, `~/workspace/portfolio-v2-brief.md`, `~/workspace/user/files/arsenal-theme-spec.md`, `VERIFICATION.md`, and the four skills (`unslop`, `design-taste-frontend`, `hallmark`, `landing-page-design`).

## Verdict: FIX FIRST

The Arsenal system is genuinely well executed, the dark theme is complete, the components are consistent, both signature moments are real, and every hard rule passes. But the site has one systemic visual defect that touches every page: the literal "one section per screen" rule (`min-height: 100svh` on every section, plus 120px section top padding and 48px hero bottom padding) leaves large empty bands and whole empty screens across the site. On desktop the pages read sparse, not spacious. The fixes are small and CSS-local (listed under Finding 1 and 2), not a rework. Fix those, re-render mobile, then ship.

A second reason for FIX FIRST rather than SHIP: I could not independently verify mobile (see "Verification limits"). The mobile render must be confirmed before ship.

## Finding 1 (highest priority): systemic dead space on every page, the "one section per screen" rule applied literally

**What is wrong.** Every `main > section` carries `min-height: calc(var(--h, 1) * 100svh)` plus 120px top padding (`src/index.css`, base layer), and every page hero adds 48px bottom padding before the next section's 120px top padding. The result is a ~170-230px empty band between the hero sentence and the next content on **every** page, and whole empty screens inside taller sections. Measured from the 1440x900 viewport screenshots:

- Home hero: sentence ends ~y=285, next content starts ~y=470.
- Projects: sentence ends ~y=305, filter pills start ~y=505.
- Skills: sentence ends ~y=285, "One person, three branches" starts ~y=470.
- Pentrix / PowerShell / BugHunt / APK / Ai-Terminal-X detail: "Back to projects" ends ~y=377, meta grid starts ~y=575-605 (a ~230px void).
- Experience, Certifications, Contact: same ~175px band.
- Home full page (6,533px tall): the tagline is 2 lines of text inside a 100svh section (content ~100px in a ~900px section); the Featured section is `--h: 2` with top-aligned content, so the whole second screen is empty; the Proof preview and "Get in touch" sections each sit in a full viewport with ~700px of empty space below their content. Content occupies roughly 40% of the page's vertical space.
- Proof full page (5,080px tall): stat cards end, then ~700px empty before "CSCB 2026: the story in three pictures"; the story section is `--h: 2`, so after the chapter list there is ~600px empty before "Scoreboard gallery".

**Why it matters.** The taste-skill `empty-area` / `section-air` checks and the Hallmark "about 80 words per screen, no section half empty" rule both exist for exactly this. A recruiter scrolling sees mostly blank ivory; the site feels unfinished rather than calm. The plan mandated the rule, but the plan's intent was "no second section heading peeking in", not "every section must be a full viewport even when its content is two lines".

**Fix.** Keep the intent, drop the literal forcing: (a) change the base section rule from `min-height: 100svh` to a content-driven height with a modest `padding-block` (keep 120px top padding only where the section genuinely needs the air, e.g. the hero); (b) reduce the hero-to-first-section gap by cutting `.page-hero` bottom padding from 48px and/or the following section's top padding; (c) set `--h: 1` sections that hold only a heading plus two lines (tagline) to auto height with generous but bounded padding (e.g. 120px top / 120px bottom), not 100svh; (d) re-check Home and Proof full pages after the change, aiming for no band wider than ~120px between content blocks.

## Finding 2: Home hero has double top padding, content floats mid-viewport

**What is wrong.** `src/routes/Home.tsx:19` puts `pt-[120px]` on the hero's inner `.wrap`, inside a `<section>` that already gets `padding-top: 120px` from the base CSS. Total top padding is 240px, so at 1440x900 the lime rule starts at ~y=240 (nav is 64px), and the hero content (lime rule to status line) occupies only y~240-745, with empty bands above and below. It is the only route with the extra `pt-[120px]` (grep confirms a single occurrence in `src/`).

**Fix.** Remove the inner `pt-[120px]` (and the `pb-12` if the section padding already covers it) and re-check the hero at 1280x800: h1, sentence, and the primary action must still sit inside the first screen, which they comfortably will once the 120px is removed.

## Finding 3: the challenge panel says "Solved" before the visitor does anything

**What is wrong.** `src/components/ChallengePanel.tsx` renders a static "Solved" pill (sage background, green check) in the panel header, and green check circles next to all three module rows (SQL injection, Cross site scripting, Insecure direct object reference), on the default first step ("Read the request"). The visitor has not solved anything, and the panel's whole premise is an interactive three-step solve. The badge and the checks contradict the interaction and make the step tabs feel pre-completed. The panel itself is otherwise excellent (real request, real flaw, real fix step on the detail page, syntax-highlighted code, no flag literal, safe flag wording).

**Fix.** Make the badge stateful: show a neutral chip (e.g. "Try it" or "Challenge 07") on steps 1-2 and switch to "Solved" only when the visitor reaches "Prove it" (and on the detail page, after "Fix it"). Give the three module rows a neutral marker (or no marker) instead of green checks; green checks should mean "done".

## Finding 4: the Archify iframes are a foreign design system inside Arsenal pages

**What is wrong.** On `/skills` (and the same file embedded on `/projects/agentarsenal`, plus the other two diagrams) the iframe brings its own chrome: its own header duplicating the page heading ("Technical identity, skills and career tree" inside the iframe, directly under the page h2 "One person, three branches"), its own toolbar (theme toggle, "Classic" dropdown, "Still", fullscreen, a dark navy "Export" button), a dotted-grid background, and different type. The dark "Export" button and teal accents are not Arsenal tokens. The iframe's own light theme was verified only in light mode; whether it follows the page into dark mode was not render-verified (see limits), and the plan's claim that the diagram "follows the page theme via the Archify viewer toggle" relies on the visitor toggling it inside the iframe.

**Fix.** If Archify allows it, embed the diagram without its internal header and toolbar (or with them collapsed), so the page h2 and caption are the only headings; verify the embed in dark mode and, if it stays light, either force its dark theme to match the page or note the mismatch as accepted. At minimum, drop the duplicated in-iframe title.

## Finding 5: the nav subtitle is invisible at 1440, the most common desktop width

**What is wrong.** `src/components/Nav.tsx` hides the subtitle "Penetration Tester, AI x Offensive Security" below 1500px (`hidden min-[1500px]:block`). The design plan requires that subtitle on desktop and only forbids it wrapping to two lines. At 1440 (and 1024) the nav shows the name only, so the role descriptor a recruiter reads first is absent exactly where most visitors will look. Hiding it fixed the wrap/truncation bug the build agent found, but it removed the content instead of fitting it.

**Fix.** Shorten the subtitle so it fits at 1440 (e.g. "Penetration Tester" or "Penetration Tester, AI x Security"), or reclaim space (tighter nav gaps, smaller theme switch) and re-check at 1440 and 1280. Do not leave the descriptor hidden at the standard desktop width.

## Further findings (lower priority, all verified)

1. **Privacy and Terms body text is centred while every other page is left-aligned.** `src/routes/Privacy.tsx:10` and `Terms.tsx:10` use `wrap max-w-[680px]`, which centres a 680px column while the hero above it spans the full 1280px wrap starting at the left edge. It reads as a misaligned narrow block. Either left-align the legal body to the wrap edge or give the legal pages a centred hero too; do not mix the two on one page.
2. **Marker weight is inconsistent.** On Home, About, Projects, Skills, Certifications, Proof, and Contact only the topic words are marked ("Izaz Haider", "break things", "projects", "the proof"). On all six project detail pages the *entire* h1 is the marker ("PowerShell Academy", "APK Security Pipeline"), which renders as a heavy full-width lavender slab with a long lime underline. It follows the plan's letter (the topic is the project name) but not its visual rhythm. Consider marking only the distinctive part of each project name.
3. **404 and short pages leave empty viewport below the footer.** The 404 page is 662px tall at 1440x900; the footer ends mid-screen and ~240px of bare ivory follows. The footer has no `closing` class, so it does not get the base CSS `min-height: 100svh` treatment. Give short pages a `min-height: 100svh` page shell (or a flex column with the footer pushed to the bottom) so the footer sits at the viewport foot.
4. **Static star count without a fetch date.** `/projects/ai-terminal-x` shows a meta cell "STARS: 38, his most starred tool". The plan says star counts are shown only if fetched live at build time and labelled with the fetch date, defaulting to none. 38 is verified (GitHub API, 2026-11-08) but the page carries no date, so it will silently go stale. Add the fetch date or drop the number to prose.
5. **Tagline activates as one block, not word by word across a trigger line.** `src/components/Tagline.tsx` uses a single `IntersectionObserver` (threshold 0.4) that flips all words on at once, staggered only by `transition-delay: 120ms` per word. The B11 spec wants words activating one at a time as they cross a trigger line. Visually close (the stagger reads well in the full-page screenshot, "Secure" lands in periwinkle as specified), so this is a small deviation, not a defect.
6. **Scroll reveals were dropped entirely.** `src/hooks.tsx`: `Reveal` is a pass-through that renders children at rest; there is no scroll fade-in anywhere. That deviates from the plan's 3.6 (rise 32px, fade 600-800ms via IntersectionObserver), but the outcome is good: no content can ever stay hidden, and the taste-skill forbids scroll fade-ins anyway. Keep the pass-through; update the plan to match the build rather than re-adding reveals.
7. **Copy nits.** About repeats its h1 in its sentence ("I break things for a living" / "I break things for a living, and I build the tools that find the break"). About adds a fifth tagline word ("Improve it.") that Home does not have ("Understand it. Break it. Build it. Secure it."). The Contact hero sentence duplicates the Home final-CTA sentence. None is a defect on its own; together they soften the copy's precision.
8. **Body type is tighter than the Arsenal spec.** The plan fixes Body 1/2/3 at 1.0625rem / 1.78; the build defines `.body-1/2/3` at 1rem / 1.5rem. Internally consistent and readable in the screenshots, but paragraphs sit denser than the spec's airy 1.78. Either restore 1.0625rem / 1.78 or record the deviation.
9. **Trivia, not findings.** One unthrottled passive scroll listener in `Nav.tsx` (only toggles a `scrolled` boolean; negligible). The `.code-block` class is defined twice in `index.css`, the second with `!important` overriding the first to a dark ground; harmless but untidy. The light `--green` is `#456b10` vs the spec's `#4E7A12`; imperceptible.

## What is good (verified, specific)

- **Arsenal fidelity is real, not a skin.** Tokens in `src/index.css` match the spec exactly (ivory `#FAF9F5`, periwinkle `#5A45D6`, lime `#B8F34A`, lilac `#EEE8FF`, marker `#C7B8F5`, Sora 800 headings, Inter body, self-hosted woff2, no Google Fonts request). The radial bg fields are the spec's two flat fields only. No hue gradients, no glows, no gradient text anywhere (grep clean). The lime rule (48x4px) appears on every page hero via `PageHero` plus Home, and nowhere else as decoration. The marker + lime underline renders correctly on every page viewed.
- **The dark theme is a complete theme, not an inversion.** Viewed on Home dark at 1440: ink `#F2F3F5` on `#121316`, marker darkened to `#3D3670` with light text (still reads as a highlight), lime identical `#B8F34A` in both themes, code strips keep the same dark ground in both themes, kicker and buttons re-tokenised (Dark pressed state in periwinkle, Light pressed in lime). The pre-paint script validates against exactly `light`/`dark`, defaults to light, uses the OS dark preference only as a first-visit hint, saves the choice, and updates `theme-color`. The switch code (`useTheme`) sets `data-theme` on `<html>` and persists to `localStorage` key `portfolio-v2-theme`. (Persistence click test not run, see limits.)
- **One system, genuinely.** One nav (active page indicated in lilac), one footer (copyright, created-by, nav, GitHub/LinkedIn, Privacy/Terms, photo credit line), one button pair (`.btn-primary` periwinkle ground, `.btn-secondary` transparent; both press to `scale(0.98)`), one card style (16px radius, full hairline border, 24px padding, 2px hover lift), one radius scale with nested radii that add up (16px outer, 8px inner code strips), one icon set (Phosphor, 18-20px, regular weight, no emoji anywhere). No page looks like it belongs to another site. Named text styles (`.heading-1` through `.caption-5`) are defined once and used throughout.
- **Moment 1 is real.** The Home hero challenge panel is a genuine interactive piece: three steps (Read the request / Spot the flaw / Prove it), real request `GET /api/orders/1042`, syntax-highlighted code via a real tokenizer (`CodeStrip.tsx`, keyword purple, strings green, numbers orange, paths cyan, comments grey), the fix step on the PenTrix detail page, and the approved flag wording only ("flag captured", "Flags are randomly generated per install at first boot"). No flag literal anywhere.
- **Moment 2 is real.** The Proof page shows the actual CSCB screenshots whole (`object-fit: contain`, never cropped), with captions in his voice, a working stepper (Previous/Next, "1 of 3", chapter list with active state), and a gallery with a lightbox (Escape/arrows in code, focus handling is the only gap: focus is not returned to the thumbnail on close). The YesWeHack swag photo and recognition email render well. Point totals appear only beside the screenshots that show them.
- **Motion is disciplined.** One easing curve everywhere (`cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`), 300ms hovers / 600ms entrance, transform and opacity only, no `transition-all`, no `animate-pulse`/`animate-ping`, no `hover:scale`, no carousels, no parallax. The hero entrance is a single fade-rise with 60/140/220/300ms stagger. The reduced-motion block forces `.enter` visible and the tagline to full colour, and content is visible with scripting off (`.tagline-word` mutes only under the `.js` class). No looping motion anywhere.
- **Hard rules: all pass.** Greps across `src/`, `index.html`, and `dist/`: no bug bounty program names, report titles, IDs, endpoints, or reward amounts; no "269/269"; no "16.40/20" (and no 16.74 either); no "53+ certifications" (the only "53+" hits in `dist/` are inside base64 font data in the three Archify diagram files, a false positive); no CWE-434 mechanism detail (CWE-434 is not named at all; "file upload" appears only as a category word in a list); no PenTrix flag-storage disclosure (only the approved safe sentence, in a callout on the PenTrix page); JSON-LD `birthDate` is `"2007-01-28"` in `dist/index.html`; no skill percentages; no invented metrics found; the `| whoami` string appears only inside a command-injection payload example in the BugHunt payload browser, which is legitimate content for that tool.

## Skill compliance (waivers honoured)

- **Waivers applied as recorded:** Arsenal lavender/periwinkle kept over the taste-skill violet ban (flat brand colours only, no gradients or glows); Sora+Inter kept over the landing-page-design Inter ban; flat ink heading with marker kept over the B5 gradient-text rule; the uppercase kicker kept as the single Arsenal-spec exception to the Hallmark/taste no-tracked-capitals rule (it appears once per page as the page label, never as a section label).
- **Unslop:** direction is in the plan and followed; quiet chrome, one accent, one icon set, plain copy, real product moments. The two moments carry the richness; nothing else glows or decorates. No typewriter, no emoji icons, no skill bars, no invented claims.
- **Hallmark Floor:** tokens at `:root`, no `transition-all`, no italic in headings, `overflow-x: clip`, `minmax(0, 1fr)` grid children, `:focus-visible` instant, reduced-motion fallback, no invented metrics, no eyebrow, no middle-dot meta strings (commas used), no purple-to-blue gradient, no card-in-card, no emoji icons, hero headline + sentence + primary action visible in the first screen at 1440x900. Deviations noted above (body line-height 1.5 vs 1.78; kicker exception is the recorded waiver).
- **Landing-page-design:** one offer, one audience, one primary action ("Get in touch", repeated identically); named text styles; B2 spacing scale (only one off-scale value found, the Home `pt-[120px]`, which is Finding 2); B3 nested radii; B4 full borders; B6 Phosphor icons; B7 base curve and IntersectionObserver tagline; B9 states present (hover/active/focus; loading skeleton and inline errors are in the contact form code, not click-tested); B10 ship requirements present (privacy/terms, branded 404, form validation, skip link, favicon, meta/OG, alt text, way back from every page via nav and "Back to projects"); B11 tagline present with the per-word stagger caveat in Finding 5.
- **Taste-skill house rules:** no caption is used to apologise for a picture (captions on Proof are evidence captions required by the plan, not decoration); every visual rendered in the sweep; equal left/right edges hold everywhere except the Privacy/Terms centred column (Finding, lower priority); one section per screen is the rule this build follows too literally (Finding 1).
